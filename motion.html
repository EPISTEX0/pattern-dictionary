Motion

Motion effects that respond to scrolling, tapping and hovering, or play when content enters the view. Every demo runs live; the keywords under each pattern describe the effect in a prompt.

Scrub (scroll-linked)

Animation tied to scroll position. Animation progress equals scroll progress: stop scrolling and the animation stops, scroll back and it plays in reverse, like dragging a video scrubber. Scroll down inside the frame, then back up.

Scroll down ↓, then back up ↑ Reduced motion: shows the end state (100%). This browser does not support scroll-driven animations.

Progress = scroll position

The ring, the progress bar and the orange block all read the same value. Scroll back up to rewind.

Use whenusers should control an animation directly: rotating a 3D product, drawing a line, a reading progress bar, video that scrubs with scroll.

Avoid whenthe animation only makes sense when it completes: users who stop scrolling midway see a half-finished state.

Stagger

Items that enter one after another. Elements in a group appear in sequence, each delayed a few tens of milliseconds after the previous one, forming a continuous wave instead of appearing at once. Press Replay to compare the two rows.

Reduced motion: shows the end state.
no stagger: all items appear at once
stagger: each tile delayed 80 ms

Use whenrevealing card grids, lists, menus or individual letters: the entry order guides the eye through the structure of the content.

Avoid whena list has dozens of items: the total wait becomes too long. Apply it to the first 6–10 items only.

Morph

A shape that transforms between states. An element changes shape continuously into its new state instead of disappearing and being replaced. Users can tell it is the same component in a different state. Tap the Place order button or the menu icon.

Reduced motion: states change instantly.
tap: button → spinner → checkmark
tap: ☰ turns into ✕
shape morphs continuously

Use whena button changes state (submit → processing → done), or for menu/close and play/pause icons: it keeps a visual link between the two states.

Avoid whenthe two states are unrelated in meaning: the morph implies a connection that does not exist.

Text reveal / Split text

Text that appears piece by piece. A heading is split into characters or words; each piece slides up from behind a mask on a staggered rhythm. Press Replay to play it again.

Reduced motion: text is visible from the start. Line 1: split characters + mask. Line 2: split words + blur.

Interfaces in motion.

Each character slides up from behind a hidden edge, one after another on a staggered rhythm.

Use whena hero heading, slogan or intro screen has one short line that needs emphasis.

Avoid whenthe text is a long paragraph or body copy: splitting slows reading, and screen readers misread it without an aria-label.

View transitions

Screen changes with shared elements. When the screen changes, elements present on both screens (image, title) move continuously from their old position to their new one instead of cutting abruptly. Tap a card.

Reduced motion: screens change instantly.

The image and title moved here from the small card. The browser captures both states and interpolates position and size.

Use whenmoving from list to detail, thumbnail to full image, or between tabs and pages in an app: users see where they came from.

Avoid whenthe two screens share no elements: the effect is reduced to a cross-fade that adds no information.

Micro-interactions

Brief feedback for a single action. A very short animation (under 0.5 seconds) confirms that an action was registered and had an effect. Tap each control in the frame.

Reduced motion: states change instantly.
like: heart bounces and bursts particles
toggle: springy knob that stretches while pressed
checkbox: the checkmark draws itself
copy: icon turns into a check, label reports the result

Use whenan action is frequent (like, toggle, check, copy, send) and users need to know at once that it succeeded.

Avoid whenthe effect is long or showy enough to block the next action: micro-interactions must be fast and stay out of the way.
