Navigation

Patterns that tell people where they are, what else there is, and where they can go next. Every demo works with a mouse, touch, and keyboard (Tab, Enter, Esc, arrow keys), like a real product.

Hamburger / Off-canvas menu

Off-canvas menu. A three-line button (☰) opens a drawer that slides in from the left edge and covers the page. The content behind it is dimmed and made non-interactive (inert), so Tab cannot reach it. Close the drawer with Esc, the ✕ button, or a tap on the dimmed area; focus then returns to the ☰ button. Click or tap ☰, or Tab to it and press Enter.

With the menu open, keep pressing Tab: focus cycles only inside the drawer.

Use whenthe screen is narrow (mobile) and the menu has more than 4–5 items, or for rarely used items such as Settings, Help, and Sign out. The drawer can also hold account details and grouped items.

Avoid whenthere are only 3–5 main destinations that people switch between often: hiding them behind ☰ reduces how often they are used. Use a bottom tab bar instead, or show the menu openly on desktop.

Prompt tip: state the slide direction, whether an overlay sits behind the drawer, and ask for “trap focus, close on Esc, return focus to trigger”. AI tools often miss these three requirements.

Mega menu

Large dropdown menu. An item in the navigation bar opens a wide panel divided into titled columns; each link has a one-line description, and the panel includes a featured content card. When opened with a mouse, the menu waits for a short delay to prevent accidental opening. Keyboard: ← → move between top-level items, ↓ enters the panel, ↑ ↓ move between links, Esc closes. Hover over or tap “Product” to open it.

Breadcrumbs

Hierarchical path. A short row of links shows the current location in a hierarchy and lets people jump up to any level in one step. When the path is too long, the middle levels collapse into a “…” button. Open a folder to go deeper, select a level in the path to go back up, or press “…” to reveal the hidden levels.

    Use whencontent is organized as a multi-level tree: folders, product categories, documentation. It is especially useful when people land directly on a deep page from a search engine or a shared link.

    Avoid whenthe site is flat with only one or two levels, or the flow is sequential (checkout, sign-up): those need a step indicator (stepper), not breadcrumbs. Do not use breadcrumbs as a replacement for a history-based Back button.

    Sidebar navigation

    Collapsible side navigation. A vertical list of destinations along the left edge of the app, with groups and nested items. A button in the corner collapses the sidebar into an icon-only strip (icon rail) to make room for content; labels then appear as tooltips on hover or keyboard focus. On narrow screens, the expanded sidebar overlays the content like a drawer. Press the collapse button, select items, and open the “Projects” group.

    Pagination

    Numbered pages for long lists. A long list is split into numbered pages with Previous and Next buttons; the current page is highlighted, and “…” stands in for distant pages. Each page has its own address (see the URL bar), so it can be shared or revisited at the same position. Select a page number or Previous/Next, or Tab to the buttons and press Enter.

    Team documents

        Use whenpeople look for a specific item, need to return to the same position, or compare and reference items (“it is on page 3”): search results, admin tables, order lists. The footer stays reachable.

        Avoid whenthe content is meant for continuous browsing (photo feeds, social media), or on mobile when the number buttons are too small to tap accurately. Do not show 20 page numbers at once; keep the first page, the last page, the pages around the current one, and “…”.

        Infinite scroll / Load more

        Infinite scroll or Load more button. Two ways to continue a long list without pages. Left column (infinite scroll): near the end, the list loads more items automatically, but the footer keeps getting pushed down. Right column (load more): people press “Load more” themselves, focus moves to the first new item, and the footer stays within reach. Scroll the left column to the end, then press the button in the right column.

        Use whenInfinite scroll: the content is for continuous browsing with no specific goal (feeds, photos, short videos). Load more: product lists, search results, and comments, when the footer is still needed and people want to control how much content loads.

        Avoid whenpeople need to return to an exact position, compare items, or count them (use pagination). Infinite scroll combined with a footer that holds important links is a common mistake: people cannot reach the footer.

        Prompt tip: for infinite scroll, name the “IntersectionObserver sentinel” and “aria-busy while loading”; for load more, ask to “move focus to first new item” and show a 6 / 30 counter so people know how many items remain.
